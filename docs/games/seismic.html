Ripple Effect

Fill every shape with the numbers 1 to its size. Two equal numbers in a line need that many cells between them.

Play Ripple EffectRuns in your browser, nothing to install.

How to play

Tap a cell to select it, then tap a number to write it. Tap the same number again, or the eraser, to clear the cell. The hint button writes one number the clues prove.

  1. Look at the shapes, not at the numbers. A shape with four cells holds 1, 2, 3 and 4 exactly once.
  2. Read the ripple rule as a no-entry zone as wide as the number: a 1 keeps another 1 out of the four cells beside it, a 3 keeps another 3 out of the three cells either side, because two 3s need three cells between them.
  3. Count the room a number needs, not just the cells next to it. On a line of six cells a 3 in the second cell can only meet another 3 in the last, because it needs three cells of gap.
  4. When a shape has only one cell left that can hold a number, that cell is it. Write it and the ripple clears its neighbours.
  5. The board is finished when every cell holds a number and no digit sits too close to its twin. A number that breaks the ripple shows in red.

Strategy

  • A 1 is the most useful clue on the board: it rules out its four orthogonal neighbours everywhere, and a shape of size one is simply that number.
  • Count the room a digit has along a line, not just beside it: two equal digits need one more cell of gap than the digit itself, so a 2 needs two cells between it and its twin and a 4 needs four.
  • Cross off by shape and by line at once: a digit placed in a shape is gone from that shape everywhere, even in cells far away.
  • The largest region's size is the highest digit on the board. Nothing above it exists, so a cell in a smaller shape can never take it.

What it asks of you

Fill every shape with the numbers 1 to its size, once each, so that two equal numbers in a line have at least that many cells between them.
