Mathrax

Fill a Latin square obeying every corner rule.

Play MathraxRuns in your browser, nothing to install.

How to play

Tap a cell to write the next number, and keep tapping to cycle back to empty. The hint button writes one number the clues prove.

  1. The board is a Latin square before it is arithmetic. A number written in a cell is gone from the rest of its row and its column, and that alone fills a lot of a five by five.
  2. Read a corner clue as two separate questions. A +7 between four cells says the top left and the bottom right add to seven AND the top right and the bottom left add to seven. Either one failing breaks the clue.
  3. Use the small numbers first. A ×2 forces a 1 and a 2, a −1 forces two neighbours, and an E or an O decides the parity of four cells at once.
  4. When a row has only one cell left for a number, that cell is it, even if you have not looked at the corners yet.
  5. The board is finished when every row and column holds each number once and no corner is red. A number that repeats or a corner that fails shows in red.

Strategy

  • Count the parity first: an E corner needs four even numbers, and on a five board that is 2 and 4 with one of them twice, so the corner immediately says which cells cannot hold 1, 3 or 5.
  • For a product clue, list the pairs that multiply to it before touching the board. A ×12 on a six board is 2 and 6 or 3 and 4, and which pair it is usually falls out of the row.
  • A × with the biggest number is the strongest clue on the board: it pairs the largest digit with a 1 or a 2, and there are very few places for either.
  • Division is the weakest of the four operations: a ÷2 means one number is twice the other, which is 1 and 2, 2 and 4, or 3 and 6 on a six board. List the pairs before you eliminate anything, and prefer a product clue when both are on the same corner.
  • Work from the corners inward. The four corner cells of the grid touch only one clue each, so they have the least to satisfy, and they feed the row and column rules for everything else.

What it asks of you

Fill every row and column with the numbers 1 to the side of the board, once each. The clue on each corner binds the four cells around it along both diagonals: E means all four are even, O means all odd, = means each diagonal pair is equal, and a number is the result of its operation on each diagonal pair.

Questions

Is Mathrax free?

Yes. Puzzlestack is free to download, with no advertising, no account and nothing collected about you. Every puzzle can be played at least once.

How long does one Mathrax take?

A few minutes at the size it opens; bigger boards are in the library.

Do I have to guess in Mathrax?

No. Where a puzzle has one answer, the generator proves it before dealing, and the clues are enough to get there by reasoning.
